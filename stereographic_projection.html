Molecular Symmetry & Stereographic Projection

Interactive Visualization for Chemical Group Theory | Dr. Snehasis Banerjee

Concept Overview:
In chemical group theory, understanding how three-dimensional symmetry operations—such as proper rotations (Cn), reflections (σ), inversion (i), and improper rotations (Sn)—generate sets of equivalent points in space is essential for assigning molecular point groups and constructing character tables.

Idea Behind Stereographic Projection

Visualizing multiple intersecting symmetry planes and rotation axes in three dimensions can be challenging on a flat page or blackboard. Stereographic projection solves this problem by projecting a three-dimensional sphere onto a two-dimensional equatorial plane while preserving angular relationships.

Imagine placing a molecule or point-group origin at the center of a reference sphere:

  • Upper Hemisphere Poles (•): A point on the upper hemisphere is connected by a straight line to the South Pole; where this line pierces the equatorial plane, it is marked as a solid dot (•).
  • Lower Hemisphere Poles (°): A point on the lower hemisphere is connected to the North Pole; where this line intersects the equatorial plane, it is marked as an open circle (°).
  • Horizontal Mirror Plane (σh): Represented by a solid outer bounding circle on the equatorial plane (dashed if σh is absent).
  • Vertical & Dihedral Planes (σv, σd): Appear as straight solid diameters passing through the center of the stereogram.
